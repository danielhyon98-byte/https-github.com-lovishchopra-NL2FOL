# Inspection

## 1. Scope

This document says what to check on each transfer. It applies to every transfer in every dossier. Walk the transfers in `seq` order. On each one, run the common checks (§3), then the checks for its `kind` (§4 to §9). On a transfer that delivered a report, also check every translation of its claims (§10). In informed mode, when several agents answered one input, also check each consolidation and every repeated fault (§11). On an answer of a science, mathematics or computer science specialist, also read its claims, translations and formal checks by §12.

**It covers:**

- the three questions you ask of every transfer
- the common checks: status, summary, delivery, digests, identifiers
- the checks for each kind of transfer: `request`, `response`, `response_text`, `relay_forward`, `relay_report`, `relay_synthesis`
- the checks for every translation of a delivered claim
- the checks for a consolidation of several answers to one input, and for repeated faults
- how to read a STEM answer: its claims, the numbers and units of its translations, its formal checks and its ST codes
- what is not a fault

**It does not cover.** Other documents handle these:

- **Kinds and codes:** which kind to give a fault you found.
- **Evidence rule:** how to pin it to the dossier.
- **Examples:** worked transfers with their correct verdicts.
- **When unsure:** a fault you cannot pin down.

## 2. Quick reference

1. Ask the three questions of every transfer (I1).
2. A fault the receipt already names is not a miscommunication, if the status follows from it (I3).
3. A REJECTED transfer was not delivered. It must not appear later as forwarded, cited or pooled (I5).
4. A forward must carry the same `payload_sha256` as the delivery of its report (I40).
5. Every delivered claim must be carried by `original_response` (I21).
6. A quotation must occur in the document it cites (I22).
7. A display status is never stronger than its signed result (I26).
8. A synthesis must account for every material claim and name every contradiction (I60 to I66).
9. Typographic differences are not faults (I9).
10. Every translation must say what its claim says, from the claim and the claims it cites; a conclusion found among its own premises proves nothing (I70 to I81). A part of the claim left without a premise is a missing link the translator exposes, not a fault (I81).
11. Several answers to one input: check each group, exclusion and conflict of the consolidation, and accuse an unnamed repeated fault on every transfer that carries it (I82 to I89).
12. A STEM answer: numbers the translator is told to write exactly, units it is told to record as an assumption, never inside an expression (instructions no code checks), formal checks that may expect UNSATISFIABLE or SATISFIABLE (I90 to I97), and a receipt whose checks and ST codes must agree with their results (I98).

## 3. Every transfer

- **I1 The three questions.** Ask them of every transfer:
  1. Did what was asked arrive, and did what was said arrive?
  2. Does the receipt say exactly what happened?
  3. Is every engine result cited for the problem it names?
- **I2 Status follows from the findings.** Check the receipt's `status` against its own contents:
  - **Request receipts:** REJECTED when any finding has severity `error`; otherwise ACCEPTED, even with warnings. A request receipt is never ACCEPTED_WITH_FINDINGS.
  - **All other receipts:**
    1. REJECTED when any finding has severity `error` and a code that starts with one of: `INT-HANDOFF`, `INT-ENV`, `INT-REQ-000`, `INT-REQ-001`, `INT-REQ-002`, `INT-REQ-009`, `INT-LEDGER-000`, `INT-LEDGER-008`.
    2. Otherwise ACCEPTED_WITH_FINDINGS when there is any finding at all, or any structural finding of severity `error` or `warning`, or any outcome whose `verification` is present with a status that is not definitive.
    3. Otherwise ACCEPTED.
  - A status that does not follow: kind `receipt_status_wrong`.
- **I3 A named fault is not a miscommunication.** When the receipt already carries a finding for a fault (for example INT-REQ-003 naming the claim whose quotation is not found) and the status follows from the findings (I2), the receipt said exactly what happened. Do not accuse it. Review the rest of the transfer.
- **I4 The summary matches the fields.** The `summary` must agree with the structured fields:
  - the status word in the summary equals `status`;
  - every code the summary lists under "Findings" is in `findings`, and every code in `findings` is listed;
  - every receipt except a request receipt ends with "Formal results concern only the encoded checks under their premises; coverage is heuristic."
  - A summary that contradicts the fields in a way only an edit explains: kind `receipt_altered`.
- **I5 Not delivered means not used.** A REJECTED transfer was not delivered. Later in the dossier it must not be:
  - forwarded (a `relay_forward` of its `report_id` with an ACCEPTED or ACCEPTED_WITH_FINDINGS status),
  - cited by a synthesis as a delivered report,
  - pooled (its claims in a later `pool_after`).
  - Any of these: kind `receipt_status_wrong`. An accepted report that never appears as delivered when it should is the same kind.
- **I6 Same payload, same digest.** Two transfers that carry the same report or request carry the same `payload_sha256`. When the dossier shows two copies of one transfer's content (a delivery and a forward of one `report_id`, a report and a synthesis quoting its text), compare them. Copies that differ: the kind of the transfer type (I40, I45, I60) or `payload_altered`.
- **I7 Identifiers resolve.** Every identifier a receipt names (in findings, coverage, constraint results, outcomes, formal checks, `formal_result_ids`) should appear somewhere in the dossier. Look it up in `index`. An identifier that resolves nowhere: `receipt_altered` for a claim, report or message id; `result_out_of_task` for a result id.
- **I8 Cited results exist.** Every id in `cited_result_ids` should be a key of `results`. A missing one: kind `result_out_of_task`.
- **I9 Not faults.** These are not miscommunications. Never accuse them:
  - typographic differences between a claim and the response: curly or straight quotes and apostrophes, dash types, extra spaces or line breaks, markdown emphasis (`*`, `_`, `` ` ``), heading hashes and list bullets;
  - a claim that is a shortened tail of the response, where the claim text is the start of what remains;
  - a claim's `certainty` or `proposed_claim_kind` you disagree with;
  - whether a claim, reading, date or figure is true;
  - a status that is a non-result (UNKNOWN, TIMEOUT, NOT_ELIGIBLE and the others), when the receipt shows it as a non-result.

## 4. `request` (router to specialist)

- **I10 The right recipient.** `recipient.agent_id` (and `payload.to_agent_id`) must be in `task.plan.selected_agent_ids`. The request's `subject` and `task_type` should match `plan.agent_subjects` and `plan.agent_task_types` for that agent. A mismatch the receipt does not name: kind `finding_missing`.
- **I11 Documents in scope.** Each `supplied_texts[].document_id` should be in `plan.allowed_document_ids` when that list is not empty. A document out of scope with no finding: kind `finding_missing`.
- **I12 Peer documents.** A supplied text whose `document_id` starts with `peer:` must be byte-identical to the `original_response` of a report that was forwarded **earlier** (lower `seq`) to **the same recipient**. Find the forward in `links.peer_documents` and compare the texts word by word.
  - The text differs, even by one word: kind `peer_document_altered`.
  - No earlier forward to that recipient: kind `peer_document_altered`.
  - The document's `author` is not the `agent_id` of the report whose text it is (or its `peer:<id>` names another forwarded report): the attributions were swapped. Kind `peer_document_altered`.
  - The same rules hold for a supplied text under **any other** `document_id` that presents another specialist's report: its `author` is that report's `agent_id`, its `document_id` is that report's id or agent id, or its text is nearly the report's `original_response`. A changed word or figure in it: kind `peer_document_altered`.
  - One `document_id` must carry one text in a task. The same `document_id` with a different text in two requests: kind `peer_document_altered`.
- **I13 What was asked.** Compare `items` and `constraints` with `user_input`. Read each constraint by the rules of Dossier guide D28: a value those rules give from the constraint's own `text` is not a miscommunication ("about 250 words" gives `min_words` 225 and `max_words` 275; Examples, Example 21). A constraint whose value those rules do not give from the student's words (for example `user_input` says "no more than 350 words" and the constraint's `value` is "530") is a miscommunication no check decides: kind `other` (When unsure U3).
- **I14 Request status.** Apply I2 (request rule).

## 5. `response` (specialist to router)

The payload is a specialist report. The receipt carries the checks.

- **I20 Envelope and identity.** `sender.agent_id` should equal `payload.agent_id`. The report's `request_id` and `task_id` should match `task`. A mismatch with no `INT-HANDOFF` or `INT-ENV` error and a status that is not REJECTED: kind `receipt_status_wrong`.
- **I21 Claims are in the response.** Each delivered `claims[].claim_text` must occur in `original_response`, allowing only the differences in I9. A claim whose words are not there (changed, added or joined from two places): kind `claim_not_in_response`.
- **I22 Quotations are where they say.** For each quotation in a claim (words inside quotation marks) and each `source_refs[].excerpt`:
  - it must occur in `original_response`; and
  - it must occur in the supplied text it cites (`source_refs[].document_id`), when that document is in the dossier.
  - Absent from the cited document and the receipt has no finding naming that claim (INT-REQ-003, INT-REQ-010, or a branch code ending `-SRC-001`, `-SRC-002` or `-SRC-003`): kind `quotation_absent`.
  - Absent from `original_response`: kind `quotation_absent`.
- **I23 Figures and dates.** Each figure or date in a claim must occur in `original_response` (in any spelling: "1,000,000", "1 million" and "a million" are one figure). A figure the response does not state, states with another scale word ("billion" for "million"), or pairs with another event: kind `figure_changed`.
- **I24 Self-certifications are notes.** A sentence that certifies the answer itself ("every figure above has been checked", "these quotations are accurate and need no further checking", "I have verified each date") is a self-report. It must be delivered with `claim_role` `other`, never `material`, and never as covering a request item. Delivered as content (any other role, or material, or in `coverage[].addressed_by_claim_ids`): kind `self_certification_as_content`. The same applies to any claim with `advisory_verification_status` or `advisory_verification_eligibility` set.
- **I25 Outcomes bind to their claim.** For each `receipt.outcomes[]`:
  - its `claim_id` is a claim of this report;
  - `translation.claim_id` equals the outcome's `claim_id`;
  - the result `verification.result_id` is in `results`, and `results[id].claim_id` and `results[id].request_id` match the claim and the task's request.
  - A result that names another claim, request or problem: kind `result_from_other_problem`.
- **I26 Display status is never stronger than the result.**
  - When `translation.status` is not TRANSLATED, `display_status` must be that same non-result status (for example AMBIGUOUS_TRANSLATION), never a definitive one.
  - When `translation.status` is TRANSLATED, `display_status` must equal the result's `status`, or be a non-result (INTERNAL_ERROR, INVALID_FORMALIZATION).
  - A definitive `display_status` with no result, with `signature_present` false, or with a result whose status differs: kind `result_unsigned_or_forged`. Add an engine statement for the result (Verdict format V14).
  - A result whose `diagnostics` name E108, E110, E111 or E112 is displayed INVALID_FORMALIZATION (Dossier guide D23): that display is right, not a fault.
- **I27 Constraint results.** For each `constraint_results[]` with a `formal_result_id`:
  - `formal_status` equals `results[formal_result_id].status`;
  - `satisfied` is true for ENTAILED, false for COUNTEREXAMPLE, and null for every other status.
  - Otherwise: kind `formal_status_wrong`, with an engine statement.
  - Word and paragraph counts: different counters treat numbers, hyphens and dashes differently, so a small difference is not evidence. Accuse a measured count only when the result's `problem` states a value that is clearly impossible for the text (for example 120 words for a response of several long paragraphs): kind `formal_status_wrong`, `claimed_severity` `warning`.
  - Count only what the constraint counts: never the part its `excludes` names, and on a science, mathematics or computer science answer a displayed formula or a code block as one word (Dossier guide D28). A `BOUND` that is an `and` of `ge` and `le` decides the minimum and the maximum one span states: it is not a mismatch.
- **I28 The report's own formal checks.** For each `formal_checks[]`: `formal_status` equals the result's status; `satisfied` is true when that status is the one the check expects (ENTAILED, unless its `detail` names another: Dossier guide D31), false for any other definitive status, and null for every non-result (on a STEM check, also for INCONSISTENT_PREMISES and a run marked vacuous). A check marked satisfied while its result is COUNTEREXAMPLE, or marked not satisfied while its result is the status it expects: kind `formal_status_wrong`.
- **I29 Coverage names real claims.** Every id in `coverage[].addressed_by_claim_ids` is a claim of this report (I7).
- **I30 Pool after.** After an accepted response, `pool_after` may gain this report's claims; after a REJECTED one it must not (I5).
- **I31 Supplied texts carried over.** A supplied text in the report under a `document_id` the request supplied must be identical to the request's text. A changed one: kind `peer_document_altered`.

## 6. `response_text` (raw text from a specialist)

The hub received the specialist's text and the channel parsed it into a report.

- **I35 Raw text is the response.** When the transfer is delivered, `raw_text` must equal `payload.original_response`. A difference: kind `payload_altered`.
- **I36 Withheld text.** When the payload is `{"withheld": {...}}`, the text was refused and not parsed. The status must be REJECTED. Nothing from it may be delivered, forwarded, cited or pooled later (I5).
- **I37 Everything else.** Run I20 to I31 on the parsed report, then §10 on its translations.

## 7. `relay_forward` (router forwards a report to another agent)

The router may pass on a delivered report only exactly as it was delivered.

- **I40 Forward equals delivery.** Find the delivery of the same `report_id` (`links.forwards`). Its `payload_sha256` must equal the forward's `payload_sha256`. If the digests differ, compare `original_response` and `claims` to find what changed.
  - The channel REJECTED the forward with INT-HANDOFF-013: the channel caught it (I3). Do not accuse the forward. Check that nothing from it was used later (I5, I12).
  - The forward is ACCEPTED or ACCEPTED_WITH_FINDINGS although it differs: kind `forward_altered`, `claimed_severity` `error`.
- **I41 Forward of a delivered report.** The forwarded `report_id` must have been delivered earlier (a `response`, `response_text` or `relay_report` with a status that is not REJECTED, with lower `seq`). Not delivered, or delivered REJECTED, and the forward was not REJECTED: kind `forward_altered`.
- **I42 Sender is the router.** `sender.role` is `router` and `contract` is `specialist_report_forward/1.0`.

## 8. `relay_report` (a specialist relays its own report)

- **I45 Owner sends.** `sender.agent_id` must equal `payload.agent_id`: only a report's owner relays it. Otherwise, with no `INT-HANDOFF` or `INT-ENV` error and a status that is not REJECTED: kind `receipt_status_wrong`.
- **I46 Checked like a response.** Run I21 to I31 on it, then §10 on its translations.
- **I47 Delivered before.** The same agent must have delivered this report earlier: a `response` or `response_text` with the same `report_id`, the same content (the same `payload_sha256`, or a payload that differs only in its `created_at`) and the same `sender.agent_id`, with lower `seq`. A report relayed in an agent's name that the agent never delivered (a new `report_id`, or changed content) was written by someone else: kind `forward_altered`, `claimed_severity` `error`.

## 9. `relay_synthesis` (the combinator's synthesis)

- **I60 Cited reports were delivered first.** Every `source_report_ids[]` must have been delivered (not REJECTED) at a lower `seq` than the synthesis. Otherwise: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`.
- **I61 Every material claim is accounted for.** Every `material` claim of the cited reports must appear in the `ledger` as carried, transformed (`paraphrased`, `merged`, `narrowed`, `generalized`) or `dropped` with a `reason`. A material claim missing from the ledger, or dropped without a reason, with no INT-LEDGER finding naming it: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`.
- **I62 Figures and dates survive.** Each figure or date in a synthesized claim must occur in its `source_claim_ids` claims, with the same scale word and paired with the same event. A changed, introduced, rescaled or re-paired figure with no INT-LEDGER-023 finding: kind `figure_changed`.
- **I63 Quotations survive.** A quotation in a synthesized claim must occur in its source claims or the cited documents. Otherwise: kind `quotation_absent` (or `synthesis_ledger_fault` when the ledger misreports it).
- **I64 Certainty is not inflated.** A synthesized claim must not be more certain than its sources: `established` built only from `probable` or `possible` sources, or "certainly" where the sources say "probably". With no finding: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`.
- **I65 Contradictions are named.** Two claims (from different specialists, or within one report) that cannot both hold under the shared meanings of their terms must be named: in `synthesis.conflicts`, or on a receipt (INT-CONFLICT-001, or INT-CONFLICT-005 for one claim that refutes itself). Unnamed: kind `contradiction_unnamed`. A difference of emphasis, interpretation or judgement is not a contradiction.
- **I66 Named conflicts are real.** A conflict a receipt or the synthesis names between claims that can in fact hold together (for example "built in 1932" and "opened in 1934", which describe different events): kind `false_conflict`.
- **I67 Required checks performed.** A `material` synthesized claim with transformation `merged` or `generalized` needs a `formal_conclusion`. Without one, the receipt must say the check was NOT performed: INT-LEDGER-011 saying "NOT performed", INT-SYNTH-012 or INT-SYNTH-006 naming the claim. None of these: kind `finding_missing`.
- **I68 Synthesis text.** The synthesis `text` should say what its `claims` say. A figure or date in `text` that no synthesized claim states (in any spelling: "1834" where the claims say "1833", "under ten" where they say "under nine"): kind `figure_changed`, `claimed_severity` `error` (the receipt must carry INT-LEDGER-023). A quotation or verdict in `text` that no synthesized claim carries: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`, `claimed_severity` `warning`. The `text` must be made of the synthesized claims, joined only by words such as "and", "however" or "finally": a sentence of `text` that is not one of the claims ("did not ban" where the claim says "banned", another Act's name, a qualifier dropped, an added sentence) with no INT-LEDGER-031 on the receipt: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`, `claimed_severity` `error`.
- **I69 Meaning is kept.** A `carried` or `paraphrased` synthesized claim with one source must not turn the source's comparison the other way ("children under nine" becomes "children over nine"; "before 1847" becomes "after 1847") or negate what the source states ("banned" becomes "did not ban"). With no INT-LEDGER-030 finding: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`. A negation that rewords a ban without changing it ("banned employing children" becomes "children could not be employed") is not this fault.
  - A `carried`, `paraphrased` or `merged` synthesized claim must not give an act to someone its sources do not name for it: a name exchanged for the source's ("Robert Peel championed the Act" where the source says "Lord Ashley championed the Act"), or a name put where the source has "he", "she" or "they" ("in October the minister resigned" becomes "in October Smith resigned" when the claim it cites never names Smith). The infrastructure checks this as INT-LEDGER-032. With no INT-LEDGER-032 on the receipt: kind `synthesis_ledger_fault`. When the source names a person and a pronoun refers to that same person within the source, check which person the claim names before you accuse.

## 10. Translations (every outcome of a `response`, `response_text` or `relay_report`)

The translator turned each eligible claim into a formal problem, and the engine decided the problem. Check that each translation says what its claim says. Choose the kind and the severity by Kinds and codes K6.

- **I70 Find each translation.** `links.translations` lists each translation the router received. For each one read, in this order: the claim (`claim_pointer`), the claims it cites (`cited_claim_pointers`), then the translation (`translation_pointer`). The translator was given only these claims and the supplied texts. A REJECTED transfer delivered no translation: skip it (Dossier guide D14).
- **I71 Status and problem agree.** A `status` of TRANSLATED or AMBIGUOUS_TRANSLATION must carry a `problem`; every other status must not. A `verification` belongs only to a translation that carries a `problem`. Otherwise: kind `translation_invalid`.
- **I72 Eligibility agrees.** When `eligibility.eligible` is false, the `status` must be NOT_ELIGIBLE and there must be no `problem`. When it is true, the `status` must not be NOT_ELIGIBLE. Otherwise: kind `translation_invalid`.
- **I73 Ambiguity is never resolved silently.** When `interpretation.ambiguity_flags` lists any flag, the `status` must be AMBIGUOUS_TRANSLATION. A flagged translation with `status` TRANSLATED: kind `translation_invalid`.
- **I74 The problem is well formed.** `problem.claim_id` equals the outcome's `claim_id`, and every `var` names a symbol listed in `symbols`. Otherwise: kind `translation_invalid`.
- **I75 Quotations are in their documents.** Every `source_refs[].excerpt` in the `grounding` of a symbol or an assertion must occur in the supplied text its `document_id` names, allowing only the differences in I9. An excerpt that document does not contain: kind `translation_ungrounded`. When its `verified` is false, the translation already disclosed it: use `claimed_severity` `warning`. When its `verified` is true, it was overstated: use `error`.
- **I76 Nothing is invented.** Every quotation in the problem must occur in the report (in `original_response`, a claim or a source excerpt) or word for word in a supplied text. Every number in the problem must be stated in the report, in any spelling: "forty" is 40, "two-thirds" is 2/3, "a tenth" may be written 1/10 or 10 per cent, "March" is 3, and a date may be written as one number such as 19020531 for 31 May 1902. The numbers 0 and 1 need no source. A premise grounded `task_setting` may take its number from the request. A quotation or number stated nowhere: kind `translation_ungrounded`.
- **I77 The conclusion is not assumed.** Take the conclusion's `expr` apart into its conjuncts: the `args` of an `and`, and through a `not` (`not (a or b)` gives `not a` and `not b`; `not (a implies b)` gives a and `not b`). A conjunct that is true whatever the symbols' values (`x eq x`, `e or not e`) needs no premise: leave it out. A premise restates a conjunct when:
  - the conjunct, or the whole conclusion, is exactly (the same JSON) the premise's `expr` or one of the `args` of a premise whose `op` is `and`; or
  - the premise says the conjunct in another form. Its `not`, `and`, `or`, `implies` and `iff` alone give the conjunct, a comparison written the other way round read as the same comparison (`gt` as a reversed `lt`, `ne` as `not` `eq`, the two sides of `eq` swapped) and `x + 0`, `x * 1`, `x - 0` and `x / 1` read as x: `e or e`, `true implies e`, `not (not e)` and `b and (b implies e)` all restate e, `105 eq q` restates `q eq 105`, and `x + 0 eq 5` restates `x eq 5`. Or the premise is the conjunct written with other comparisons of the same values: `q ge 105 and q le 105` restates `q eq 105`, and `not (x lt 3)` restates `x ge 3`. Or the premise is the conjunct rewritten by arithmetic on its own symbols, true for exactly the same values: its terms moved across, or both sides multiplied or divided by the same number, `x - 2.00 eq 0` and `2 * x eq 4.00` restate `x eq 2.00`, and `2 * x + 3 eq 7` restates `x eq 2` (rule arithmetic-restatement).
  
  Arithmetic on numbers is no restatement: a premise that gives the conjunct only by the arithmetic it writes on numbers alone (a line of working such as `a eq (20 - 0) / 4.0` beside the conclusion `a eq 5.0`, or `R * (1 / 4.0 + 1 / 6.0) eq 1` beside `R eq 2.4`) is a derivation the engine checks (I94), and so is a premise that gives the conjunct without saying the same (`x eq 5` beside `x gt 3`).
  
  Read each restating premise's `grounding`. Its `kind` is a label the translator wrote, not evidence. A restating premise is a transcription, a check of the claim against its source, only when the code verified it: its `verified` is true (the code found its excerpt in the named supplied text, and the excerpt states the premise's numbers; a `verified` the translator set for a premise with no excerpt, by its own check of the task's numbers, a `grounding_checks` entry whose `method` is `numeric_in_task` or `relation_in_source`, is a number match and verifies nothing here), its `kind` is `quotation`, `supplied_text`, `arithmetic_fact` or `task_setting`, every number in it is stated in the report (a `task_setting`'s may be stated in the request's `user_input` or `constraints` instead), and its excerpt does not state that number only as the result the answer must show ("Show that the distance is 8 m" states a target, never data), nor only where its supplied text does not state it as a statement (in a yes/no question, under a negation, as one of alternatives, inside a claim the answer must examine, or as a requirement or a limit the premise's words do not name: the next list). Do not accuse a transcription as circular (one that says something its source does not is a meaning question, I78).

  Every other restating premise is the writer's own for the checks, whatever its `kind` says: `assumption`, `specialist_inference`, `general_knowledge`, no grounding, a `verified` that is false, a `quotation` or `arithmetic_fact` whose `verified` is not true, every `rubric_rule`, `knowledge_document` or `library_source` (the checks read no premise in them), and every `task_setting` or `supplied_text` whose `verified` is not true. A number of the request that matches is no evidence: a premise that carries no excerpt is never a transcription, even when the translator marked it `verified` by its own check of the task's numbers (`numeric_in_task`, `relation_in_source`). Of those, a `task_setting` or `supplied_text` premise with no excerpt is SHOWN not to be data (the request's statement) when:
  - it is a `supplied_text` whose `source_refs` name no document of the request's `supplied_texts` (none, or one the request does not supply);
  - a number in it (other than 0 and 1) is stated nowhere in the request (its `user_input`, `constraints` and `supplied_texts`; a `supplied_text`'s: nowhere in the documents it names), in any spelling (I76; a frequency word states its periods a year: "monthly" 12, "quarterly" 4, "weekly" 52, "daily" 365, "annually" 1);
  - the request states that number only as the result the answer must show ("Show that ... is 8 m"), only for another quantity than the premise's `label` and its symbols' `meaning` name ("80 words" beside a count of the points made; "8 seconds" beside a distance; in a table, a number in a column whose header names another quantity), or only as a limit ("no more than 80 words") while the premise states it as the quantity's value and its words name no limit;
  - the request states each such number for the quantity the premise names only where it does not state it as a statement: in a yes/no question ("Does the extract mention 3 workers?", "Discuss whether ...", or a yes/no part of a question: "How many workers does it mention, or is it 3?"); under a negation (before the number, "does not mention 3 workers", "fails to mention 3 workers", "It is false that ...", or after it, one that negates what the premise says, "3 workers are not mentioned" beside "the extract mentions 3 workers"); as one of alternatives joined by "or" ("3 workers or 4 workers", "Which is right, 3 workers or 4 workers?"), which states neither; inside a claim the answer must examine ("Evaluate the claim that ...", "do you agree that ...", a sentence or a quotation followed by "Discuss." or "To what extent do you agree?"); as a requirement on the answer ("Your answer should be 80 words long", "Write 80 words", "Give 3 reasons", "in no more than 350 words"; every entry of `constraints` is one); or as the value of a limit or target ("The word limit is 80 words", "its target of 2 per cent"), while the premise's words name no requirement or limit (they say what the answer or the quantity is, not what it must be or must not exceed);
  - the translation itself does not present it as data: a symbol in it that `symbols` grounds as `assumption`, `specialist_inference` or `general_knowledge`; or, for a premise that states a `Bool` symbol, a symbol `symbols` gives no grounding, or a conclusion grounded as the writer's own or not grounded at all;
  - nothing in it is what a check reads in the request, so only its label presents it as data, and a label is no evidence: it states a `Bool` symbol, whatever `symbols` and the conclusion's `grounding` say (no check reads what a `Bool` statement means against the request, so "p proves p" under a `task_setting` or `supplied_text` label is the claim assumed); it states anything but the values of quantities (each part a comparison `eq`, `lt`, `le`, `gt` or `ge` of one `Int` or `Real` symbol with one number: no arithmetic, no comparison of two symbols, no `not`, `ne` or `or`); a number in it is 0 or 1 (never read as data); or the request states a number in it only bare, with no quantity beside it ("Question 2."), for a premise whose `label` and symbols' `meaning` name no quantity, otherwise than the premise states it (a value where it states a limit, or a limit where it states a value and its words name no limit), or, in a table, only in a row the premise's words do not name.

  Every other `task_setting` or `supplied_text` premise with no excerpt is UNDECIDED: it states only values of quantities, and the request (a `supplied_text`'s: the documents it names) states each of its numbers for the quantity its `label` or its symbols' `meaning` name, as the premise states it: right after the number ("for 5 years", "3.6%", "compounded monthly"), or, in a table, in the column whose header names that quantity and the row the premise's words name; and it states it as a statement. Data written in an instruction ("Find the value of the investment after 5 years"), a supposition ("If the car travels 8 m in 2 s, ..."), what a wh-question takes as given ("How does the temperature, from 20 °C to 60 °C, affect ...?"), a negated limit ("does not exceed 5 m") and a number restated another way after a comma and "or" ("$0.30 per litre, or $31.50 million per year") are statements; a requirement or a limit is the request's statement only for a premise whose words name one ("the word limit is 80 words"). No check verifies or refutes it. Whether the request says what it says, about the same quantity, is for you to decide (I78).

  The translator withholds some of these encodings itself, as INVALID_FORMALIZATION with E112 in `diagnostics` and no `problem` (Dossier guide D21): that is no fault to accuse. It delivers the others: its own rule reads a restatement only when it is written exactly as the conclusion, and reads a `task_setting`, `supplied_text`, `rubric_rule`, `knowledge_document` or `library_source` label as a transcription even when nothing was verified, so the engine gives ENTAILED with no E112 (Examples, Examples 26 to 30). For a delivered `problem`:
  - **Every conjunct restated.** Each conjunct that needs a premise is restated by some premise, and at least one restating premise is the writer's own (a premise that restates the whole conclusion is enough). Ask whether the other premises give the conclusion without the writer's-own restating premises:
    - they do (for example a result written out beside the working that already gives it): the restatement adds nothing, and the result does not rest on it. The checks decide this with an engine run of the problem without those premises, and report it as OVS-TRN-010, a warning. Do not accuse it (Examples, Example 22).
    - they do not, and a premise SHOWN not to be data is one the result needs (without the shown ones, keeping the undecided ones, the conclusion does not follow): the problem assumes what it should show, and ENTAILED then proves nothing about the specialist's reasoning: kind `translation_circular`, also when a transcription restates another conjunct, and whatever the outcome's `display_status` (Examples, Examples 26 to 28 and 30);
    - they do not, but the result rests only on UNDECIDED premises: the checks report OVS-TRN-011, a warning, and decide nothing, because a result that rests on its data premises shows only that a data conjunct needs its data premise. Compare each such premise with the request: when it says what the request says, about the same quantity, it is a transcription the code could not verify: no accusation (Examples, Example 29). When it does not, kind `translation_circular`; no check can confirm it, so it goes to human review.
  - **Only part restated.** Another conjunct is given by other premises (or left without one), the result's `status` is ENTAILED, and a writer's-own premise restates the rest. Read the outcome's `verification`:
    - its `ungrounded_dependencies` or `unverified_dependencies` list that premise: the receipt says the result relies on something not established. Do not accuse it; whether that part is the claim's point rather than a fact it states is a meaning question (I78);
    - neither list names it (a `task_setting`, `rubric_rule` or `arithmetic_fact` no check verified: the receipt presents it as given with the task): ask whether the other premises give that part without it. They do: no accusation. They do not: kind `translation_circular` when the premise is SHOWN not to be data; when it is UNDECIDED (OVS-TRN-011), compare it with the request as above.
- **I78 The problem says what the claim says.** This is the meaning check. Compare the conclusion (its `label` and its `expr`) with the claim, and the premises with the claims it cites. Look for:
  1. **A dropped condition.** The claim says "only if", "unless", "when", "some" or "most", or limits a time or a place, and the problem drops it.
  2. **A flipped comparison or direction.** "More than" encoded with `lt`; "at least" (`ge`) encoded as "more than" (`gt`); "before" encoded as after; "A led to B" encoded as B `implies` A.
  3. **An invented premise.** A premise that adds a fact or an intermediate claim stated nowhere: not in the claim, not in a claim it cites, and not in `original_response`. A premise that restates a claim the claim cites, and an `implies` premise grounded `specialist_inference` that takes the step from the claims the claim cites to the claim, are the specialist's own reasoning, not invented (I81; Examples, Example 14).
  4. **An ambiguity resolved silently.** The claim can be read two ways, the problem encodes one, and `ambiguity_flags` is empty.
  5. **A claim forced into a problem.** The claim needs what a problem cannot say (possibility, degree, change over time, a claim about every reader), and the problem presents it as a plain fact. A claim the eligibility policy rules out that still carries a problem is decided by the checks (OVS-TRN-002): kind `translation_invalid`. A reading, a value judgement or a thesis the policy made eligible, encoded as an argument from the claims it cites, is not this fault: the translator encodes points and theses with their supporting claims as an argument.
  6. **A conclusion that is not the claim.** The conclusion's `label` or `expr` says something narrower, broader, or about another subject than the claim.
  7. **Another stated given substituted.** A premise puts another value the report or the request states in place of the one the claim's working uses: another row of a table (the 25 °C uptake for the 20 °C one), another quantity's value (the external resistance where the claim divides by the total), a value from another part. Every number is still stated somewhere, so no check names it: read which value the claim's line of working uses.
  8. **Another relation encoded.** A premise encodes another formula or relation than the claim's line of working: another law, a rearrangement the claim does not write, the change itself where the claim states a percentage change, a division where the claim multiplies. A premise that states the claim's own working with its values substituted is the specialist's own (I94).
  9. **Two incompatible quantities combined.** A premise adds, subtracts or compares quantities of different kinds (a temperature and an uptake, a time and a length), or two values of one quantity in different units with no conversion (I92). The engine compares numbers only, so the sum is computed and no check names it.
  
  Any of these: kind `translation_unfaithful`. Give one pointer, with a quote, to the claim's words, and one pointer, with a quote, to the part of the problem that differs. No check compares meanings, so the accusation goes to human review.
- **I79 The outcome around the translation.** Apply I25 and I26 to the outcome. Its `summary` must not state more than its translation and its result: a summary that calls the claim proven, true or verified, or names another status than `display_status`: kind `receipt_altered`.
- **I80 What a result shows.** A result's status is about the encoding (Dossier guide D23). Never accuse a translation because of its result's status alone, and never treat ENTAILED as a sign that the translation is faithful.
- **I81 Not faults in a translation.** Never accuse:
  - symbol names, premise ids or labels worded differently from the claim, when the meaning is the same;
  - an encoding other than the one you would write that says the same thing;
  - a premise that restates a claim the claim cites;
  - an `implies` premise grounded `specialist_inference` from the claims the claim cites to the claim: it is the specialist's own chain of reasoning (Examples, Example 14);
  - a part (a conjunct) of the conclusion with no premise, when the report gives no support for it and the translation's `reading` leaves it unsupported: the translator exposes a missing link, and COUNTEREXAMPLE is the honest result (Examples, Example 20);
  - a missing premise that would state the claim's own fact: that premise would assume the conclusion (I77);
  - in informed mode, a definitive display that rests on supporting claims whose own outcomes are not successful, or one fact a premise in one translation and unsupported in another: the checks report these (OVS-TRN-008, OVS-TRN-009);
  - a quotation that widens the specialist's quoted words to the full phrase of the same supplied text;
  - a NOT_ELIGIBLE, UNSUPPORTED, INVALID_FORMALIZATION or TRANSLATION_FAILED translation without a problem, shown as that non-result;
  - a premise that restates the conclusion when the other premises already give it: the checks report it (OVS-TRN-010, I77);
  - a premise that restates the conclusion as a transcription a check verified: its `verified` is true and its excerpt was located (I77); nor `task_setting` or `supplied_text` premises with no excerpt on which alone the result rests, each stating only values of quantities that say what the request says about the same quantity (OVS-TRN-011 in informed mode: a transcription the code could not verify, I77). A restating premise no check verified that states a `Bool` symbol, or whose number the request states nowhere, only bare, only as a target, only for another quantity, only as a limit, or only in a yes/no question, under a negation, as one of alternatives, inside a claim the answer must examine or as a requirement on the answer, or a `quotation`, `arithmetic_fact`, `rubric_rule`, `knowledge_document` or `library_source` no check verified, is a label only, not a transcription: apply I77;
  - a writer's-own premise that restates only part of the conclusion, under an ENTAILED result whose `ungrounded_dependencies` or `unverified_dependencies` list it: the receipt discloses it (I77);
  - a premise that gives the conclusion only by the arithmetic it writes on numbers alone (a line of working): a derivation, not a restatement (I77, I94). The conclusion rewritten by arithmetic on its own symbols (`x - 2.00 eq 0` or `2 * x eq 4.00` beside `x eq 2.00`) is a restatement: apply I77;
  - an INVALID_FORMALIZATION translation whose `diagnostics` name E112: the translator withheld the circular encoding itself, and nothing was run (I77);
  - whether the claim itself is true (Never N6).

## 11. Consolidations and repeated faults

These checks apply in informed mode, when the dossier has `consolidations` (Dossier guide D24). The infrastructure made each consolidation by fixed rules; you check that it agrees with the transfers it names. A consolidation fault is decided by no check: use kind `other` with `claimed_severity` `warning` (Kinds and codes K7).

- **I82 Find each consolidation.** For each entry of `consolidations`, read `request_message_ids`, then the `groups`, the `excluded` answers, the `repeats` and the `conflicts`. Open every transfer they name at its `transfer_pointer`.
- **I83 Every answer is accounted for.** Every `response` or `response_text` whose `in_reply_to` is one of `request_message_ids` must be a member of exactly one group or be listed in `excluded`. An answer missing from both, or listed twice: kind `other`, naming its `message_id`, with one pointer to the consolidation's `at` and one to the answer's transfer.
- **I84 Members say the same thing.** In a group with `basis` `identical_text`, the members' `original_response` are the same text, apart from the differences in I9. In a group with `basis` `same_material_claims`, compare each member's material claims (`materiality` `material`, `claim_role` not `other`) with the representative's: the same claims, the same figures and dates, the same quoted words, the same "not". A member whose material claim differs in a figure, a date, a quotation, a negation or a name: kind `other`, with one pointer and quote for each of the two claims (When unsure U11).
- **I85 The representative.** `canonical_message_id` must be a member of its group. When `canonical_clean` is true, that member's `clean` is true (Dossier guide D26): its transfer's receipt `status` is ACCEPTED, or ACCEPTED_WITH_FINDINGS whose only findings are INT-REQ-016 warnings naming other answers to the same input. When `canonical_clean` is false, no member's `clean` is true and the representative is the earliest member. An earlier member whose `clean` is true should have been chosen instead. Otherwise: kind `other`.
- **I86 Faulty answers are never members.** An answer whose receipt carries a finding of severity `error`, or that `repeats` lists, must be in `excluded`, never in a group, and never be `deliverable_message_id`. A faulty answer in a group: kind `other`, naming the answer, with a pointer to its error finding and one to the group.
- **I87 Conflicts are disclosed, never chosen.** With two or more groups, or any entry in `conflicts`, the `status` must be CONFLICTING and `deliverable_message_id` must be null: the router discloses the groups instead of choosing one. CONSOLIDATED or CONSOLIDATED_WITH_FINDINGS with two groups or a conflict: kind `other`. Never decide yourself which group is right (Never N13).
- **I88 Repeated faults.** A repeated fault is the same fault in a later answer of the task: the same kind of finding, about the same quoted words or the same claim words, with the same figures, from another agent, another instance of the same specialist, or the same agent resubmitting after a rejection, even reworded.
  - When the receipts name the fault and `repeats` lists the repeat, it is already recorded: do not accuse it (I3). `repeats` lists error-level faults only (Dossier guide D27).
  - When you find the same fault in several transfers and no receipt names it (for example a self-certification delivered as content, or a figure the response does not state), accuse it on every transfer that carries it: one accusation per transfer, the same kind, each with that transfer's own identifiers and evidence. Say in `statement` which earlier transfer it repeats (Examples, Example 19).
  - Never accuse a fault only because it repeats: each accusation is about one transfer.
- **I89 Not faults in a consolidation.** Never accuse:
  - answers worded differently that a group merged with `basis` `same_material_claims` and a `merge_check` whose `relation` is `consistent`;
  - answers kept in two groups with an `advisory` note: the infrastructure does not merge what the engine could not confirm;
  - a group with one member, or the number of agents in a group: agreement is never evidence;
  - which group is true, better or more complete (Never N6, N13);
  - a warning that recurs across answers (a paragraph count, a structure warning) and is not in `repeats`: `repeats` lists error-level faults only.

## 12. STEM answers (science, mathematics and computer science)

An answer of a science, mathematics or computer science specialist is checked like any other (§3 to §10). These rules say how its claims, translations and formal checks look (Dossier guide D29 to D31).

- **I90 Read the claims as statements.** Each claim is one statement of the answer, exactly as written (Dossier guide D29): a line of working, a table or a code block is one claim, and its text may hold no words at all. The same claim id names a claim in each report of one branch (Dossier guide D20): name the `report_id` and the `message_id` with every claim id you accuse.
- **I91 Numbers written exactly.** The translator is told to write every number of a STEM answer exactly, and the IR admits only an exact integer, fraction or decimal with no exponent (Dossier guide D30). These are the answer's own numbers, not invented ones:
  - a number in scientific notation, expanded: 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ as 0.0000000000667, 3.0e8 as 300000000;
  - a value with a prefixed unit, in the unit without the prefix: 15 cm as 0.15, 2.5 km as 2500, 500 g as 0.5 (kilograms);
  - a value given to a precision, as the ends of its interval: 9.77 as 9.765 and 9.775, "≈ 9.8" as 9.75 and 9.85;
  - a percentage the answer writes with its sign, as the percentage or as its fraction: 3.6% as 3.6 or as 0.036.
  
  Any other number the answer does not state is named by the checks (OVS-TRN-005, a warning): for example a conversion the translator made itself (minutes into seconds, degrees Celsius into kelvin), or a number a hundred times, or a hundredth of, one the answer states with no percent sign (a rate the answer writes 0.003, encoded 0.03). Check that the converted value is the stated one before you accuse anything: a correct conversion says what the claim says.
- **I92 Units.** The translator is told to record a unit as an assumption, never inside an expression (Dossier guide D30): look for it in words, in a symbol's `meaning`, an assertion's `label`, the problem's `notes`, the `interpretation`'s `reading` or `glossary`, or under a grounding of kind `assumption`; the engine compares numbers only. Two values of one quantity in different units (centimetres beside metres) combined with no conversion, or two quantities of different kinds combined at all (a temperature subtracted from an uptake: I78 item 9), change what the claim says: kind `translation_unfaithful`, with a pointer and a quote for each value. A value the translator converted (a figure of a quotation written under another prefix of its unit, 50.0 cm³ as 0.0500 dm³) is no grounding fault (Dossier guide D30); the checks name it as a number the answer does not state (OVS-TRN-005) unless it is in the unit without the prefix (I91): check that the converted value is the stated one.
- **I93 Significant figures and tolerances.** A result stated to a precision ("9.77", "≈ 13 N", "to 3 s.f.") may be encoded as an interval around the stated value. The interval must contain the stated value and be no wider than the precision the answer states (half a unit of its last digit), or than the tolerance it states ("within 2%" of 9.81 is 9.6138 to 10.0062). A wider interval encodes a weaker claim; an exact equality where the answer says "≈" encodes a stronger one: kind `translation_unfaithful`.
- **I94 Equations and lines of working.** The translator is told to encode a line of working with the stated values put in place of its symbols (Dossier guide D30); a value left as a symbol is no fault for that alone. Each "=" of a chain is a step the specialist wrote: the conclusion is the line's result, and the premises are the substitution and the steps before it. A premise that equates the result's symbol with the arithmetic of the given values is the specialist's own working, not the conclusion assumed (I77). Compare that premise with the line of working itself, value by value and operation by operation: a premise that substitutes another stated given, encodes another formula or rearrangement than the line writes, or combines two incompatible quantities is kind `translation_unfaithful` (I78 items 7 to 9; Examples, Example 33). No check compares them: the numbers are all stated, and the engine computes whatever relation it is given.
- **I95 Truth tables and Boolean identities.** A truth table is one claim. A Boolean identity is checked by the branch itself (Dossier guide D31): UNSATISFIABLE with `satisfied` true means that the identity holds for every input, and is not a fault (I28). A Boolean expression whose statement gives its inputs values ("With A = 1 and B = 0, NOT A OR B = 0", "Let A = 1 and B = 0. Then NOT A OR B = 0") is checked as evaluated under them: its `detail` says "the Boolean evaluation" and names the values, and UNSATISFIABLE with `satisfied` true means it holds under them (Examples, Example 36). Never read such a check as an identity, and never accuse the evaluation because the expression is no identity: the signed result decides it (I28). The gates NAND, NOR and XNOR (↑ or ⊼, ↓ or ⊽) are read as the negations of AND, OR and XOR, in identities, evaluations and truth tables alike; a gate written beside another operator, or two NAND or two NOR, with no brackets has two readings, and ST-PARSE-002 names it unchecked (Dossier guide D31, D32): no `finding_missing` for its missing check. A translation of an identity with no premise is vacuous: the engine marks it E108, and the receipt shows INVALID_FORMALIZATION, a non-result (I9).
- **I96 Mathematical items.** A solution, a root or an inequality is encoded from the equation or inequality the answer solves (often grounded `task_setting` from the request). Check the direction and the connectives: an inequality whose sign the answer reverses (dividing by a negative number) must be reversed in the encoding too, and a set of roots must be encoded as stated ("x = 2 or x = −3", never "x = 2 and x = −3"). A direction or a connective changed: kind `translation_unfaithful` (I78; Examples, Example 25).
- **I97 Not faults in a STEM answer.** Never accuse:
  - a number written exactly in place of scientific notation, a prefixed unit read without its prefix, or a stated precision written as an interval (I91); nor a figure of a quotation the translator converted correctly to another prefix of its unit (I92: OVS-TRN-005 may name it as a warning);
  - a unit recorded as an assumption, never inside an expression, as the translator is told to record it (I92);
  - a symbol not prefixed with its claim, or a stated value left as a symbol: the translator is told to avoid both, but no code requires it, and neither changes what is encoded (Dossier guide D30; judge the encoding by I78);
  - a formal check with `satisfied` true and the status its `detail` expects, UNSATISFIABLE or SATISFIABLE included (I28);
  - a check for the reading its `detail` names:
    - a substitution that holds by rule part-value although its value matches no value stated for its symbol (the line gives its part's own result, or the value is a result its part computes with the symbol's dimension: Examples, Example 37; or a value its part's prose states for a quantity named otherwise, another part's value of the quantity the line computes, or a computation written in the symbol's place);
    - a relation in prose checked by rule bound-relation, a conversion the prose states checked by rule prose-conversion, a restatement failed by rule same-place;
    - no algebra step from a rearrangement to the line that evaluates the formula (rule rearranged-formula: that line is the formula's substitution, and the rearrangement is the step from the formula), and no restatement check for a bare number after "so" that follows no chain of its own statement or of the one before (rule adjacent-restatement);
    - a symbol reused in its part with a value the request states, checked at severity warning in a check of its own (rule part-given), or read with no check where the part's question or its working names that value; a Given written in prose, checked as the same Given written with "=" (rule prose-given); and a relation read with the unit written after a fraction as the fraction's own, with no ST-DIM-001 (rule fraction-unit).
    - Nor accuse the absence of an algebra step from a law to the line right after it that evaluates the law solved for one of its symbols (rule solved-law: that line is the solved law's substitution, its `detail` saying "solved for"), or a value restated more finely than the chain's value that fails, at severity warning, the check against the chain's written arithmetic while it holds against the chain's value (rule finer-restatement).
    - Nor accuse the absence of a check between a run's condition, the last value of the list that names its quantity or an item's number and the computation after it (rules value-at, name-list and item-label: they name the quantity, and only a value the computation takes for the condition or for a listed value is checked), or a check whose `detail` says "rule law-values" that fails, at severity warning, while the line's arithmetic holds (the line does not evaluate the law its part states: Examples, Example 39), or a number the working takes beside a supplied table's cells set against a row's cell or a stated value (rule table-terms).
    - Nor accuse a check whose `detail` names one of these rules for failing at severity warning while the line's own arithmetic holds, or for setting a value against what the answer did not write itself: rule named-result (the value a line gives a quantity the request names, against the request's value of it), rule given-function (a Given of a function's value or of a function, against the request's function), rule row-terms (a term of a sum over a row's repeats, against the row's repeats), rule decay-law (a value computed over a stated number of half-lives, against the decay law), rule statistics (a median, a mode, a range or a mean the branch forms from the listed values or a supplied column, against the stated value) and rule rounded-digits (the place a stated rounding names, against the place the value is written to). Nor accuse a change stated from a part's result for being set against that part's answer rather than a check line or a line naming another quantity at the end of the part (rule part-answer).
    - Nor accuse, for the same reasons, a check whose `detail` names rule named-component (the value a line substitutes, against the component its statement names by its value) or rule condition-result (a value computed under a condition, against the value computed for its symbol under that condition before) for failing at severity warning while the line's own substitution and arithmetic hold; a check of a definite integral or of its evaluation bracket (rule definite-integral), of a decimal written with its digits cut (rule ellipsis-rounding: any value from half a unit below to a whole unit above its last written digit) or of a number restated in another base (rule prose-base); a value at a supplied table's row read in any order of its clause or at a numbered row (rule row-value); or a check that fails within 1/100 when its `detail` gives a smaller τ (rule operand-precision: the request's data are exact).
    - Nor accuse, for the same reasons, a check whose `detail` names rule derivative (a polynomial's derivative against the one the power rule forms), rule function-value (a function's value against the function the request or the answer defines), rule inequality-solution (a solved bound against the relation before it solved exactly), rule same-shape (a numeral against the request's value the answer's other evaluation of the expression writes there) or rule unbound-substitution (a value against the request's value the answer substitutes for the symbol elsewhere), each a warning where it rests on a reading; a check that holds a rounded quotient of whole numbers to its last digit (rule exact-rounding) or a result to the rounding its line states (rule result-rounding); a Given or a substituted value failed one unit off the request's data (rule given-data: the data are taken as written); or the roots and steps of an equation in a letter another part's question gives a value (rule part-condition).
    - Nor accuse, for the same reasons, an identity check whose `detail` says it is decided for every value of its variable, no binding or formula of it included (rule identity-variable: a derivative, an antiderivative, a definite integral's evaluation bracket, a Given function, a factorisation), for failing although the request gives that variable a value; a numeral with letter digits read in base 16 where the words beside it name that base, or a numeral set equal to one whose base the words after it name (rule prose-base); or a value restated after an approximation word, with τ 0 (rule inline-restatement). An identity check whose `problem` gives its variable a value is no such check (I98);
    - The signed result decides each (I28; Dossier guide D31);
  - a STEM check shown undecided (INT-SUBJ-002), or failed at severity warning (INT-SUBJ-004), when its `detail` says why;
  - a derivative of what is no polynomial the answer or the request defines, in Leibniz notation of any order or as a letter with primes written applied to a bracket ("dN/dt = −λN"), named ST-UNDEC-001 and not checked; a polynomial's derivative ("d²v/dt² = −6", "v'(t) = 6 − 6t") is set against the one the power rule forms (rule derivative), and a derivative's value at a point against a formula the answer states for that derivative (Dossier guide D31, D32);
  - an ST code: the receipt names what it says (I3). Most ST codes say what the branch did not check, or checked under a limitation; ST-DIM-001, ST-DIM-002 and ST-UNDEF-001 say the relation is wrong as written (its dimensions disagree, a sum adds unlike quantities, an expression is undefined), decided by the branch's own unit arithmetic or domain rule (Dossier guide D32);
  - a STEM claim for its silence alone: with no check and no ST code the branch decided nothing about it (Dossier guide D31), and whether it holds is not a transfer question (Never N6). Never read the silence as checked either. Only a Given value the receipt leaves with neither its check nor its ST-UNDEC-003 is a fault, of the receipt (I98);
  - whether a law, a constant or a result is physically or mathematically true (Never N6).
- **I98 A STEM receipt.** Check the receipt's formal checks and ST codes against each other and against their results (Dossier guide D31, D32):
  - a check whose `satisfied` is false has an INT-SUBJ-001, or for a check whose `detail` says "failure severity warning" an INT-SUBJ-004, naming its `formal_result_id`. None: kind `finding_missing`. With it, the fault is named: do not accuse it (I3). The finding of a failed substitution also names, after the claims of its statement, the claims that state the values it substitutes for (Dossier guide D31): those claims are not a moved finding;
  - the status a check's `detail` ends with ("solver COUNTEREXAMPLE") is its result's `status`, its claims are the claims of the statement it quotes, and the c and r of its failure finding are the values its result's `problem` gives. A `detail` or a finding that states another status, other claims or other values than its result: kind `receipt_altered`, with a pointer to the `detail` or the finding and one to the result;
  - `formal_status` or `satisfied` that disagree with the result are I28 (`formal_status_wrong`);
  - an identity check (its `detail` says it holds "for every value of" its variable: rules derivative, antiderivative, definite-integral and given-function, and rule identity-variable's own words) whose `problem` gives that variable a value or a formula (a premise `B_…` or `FORMULA` of it; its `detail` says "bindings included") and whose result is the success it expects was decided at that value only, not for every value: kind `formal_status_wrong` (the checks report OVS-FORM-005, rule identity-statement; Dossier guide D31). A constant the identity does not quantify may take a value;
  - a Given value (Dossier guide D31: a symbol or a named quantity given a number with "=" in a statement that begins "Given", "Data", "Known" or "We know", or in the list a line "Given:" opens; or a value the prose gives a quantity in a statement whose head ends with a colon, "Data: emf 15 V, internal resistance 0.50 Ω.", "Given: the mass of seeds is 12 g.", "Given: 15.0 cm³ of acid.", in the list a line "Given:" opens, or in the first clause, up to the first comma, semicolon or colon, of a statement that begins "Given", "Known" or "We know" with no such colon, "We know the mass of seeds was 10 g."; never a value right after "at", "when", "whenever" or "if", the condition it is stated under) of a report that answers a request has its own check, one of these:
    - a `formal_checks` entry naming its claim whose `detail` begins "stem_given" and quotes the value ("the Given value against the request: '3.9%'"), or, for a Given of a function's value or of a function ("Given: v(1) = 4 m s⁻¹.", "Given: v(t) = 6t − 2t²."), a stem_arithmetic or stem_algebra entry whose `detail` names rule given-function and quotes the Given ("the request's function at the Given's argument, against the Given value: 'v(1) = 4 m s⁻¹'": Examples, Example 40), or for f, g or h applied to a number the written arithmetic of the application of the function the request or the answer defines ("the written arithmetic: 'f(2) = 6'"), or is listed by an ST-UNDEC-003 naming its claim ("the Given value t = 5.0 s ... could not be set against the request"), unless its value is zero or it is a data-booklet constant written at the booklet's value.
    - A Given stated under a condition ("Given: oxygen uptake at 30 °C = 80 mm³ per g per hour") quotes its value, never the condition: its check is the stem_given entry set against the supplied table's row ("the Given value against the request: '80 mm³ per g per hour'"), and the condition is no Given of its own (Examples, Example 35).
    - A Given uncertainty written with "±", "+/-" or "%" ("Given: count rate uncertainty = ±3%") is a Given value like any other: its check sets it against the uncertainties the request and its supplied texts state, and its ST-UNDEC-003 names it when they state none of its kind; the uncertainty written with a Given value ("Given: T = 20.0 ± 0.5 °C") is checked only when they state one of its kind, while the Given's central value always is.
    - Neither: kind `finding_missing`, with a pointer to the claim's text quoting the Given and a pointer to the receipt's `structural_findings` with no quote (Evidence rule E8).
    - A numeral written with a thousands space ("4 000"), or a Given in a maths span the branch cannot read whole, is ST-PARSE-001 instead; an amount written after a currency sign ("P = €4000") is no number the branch reads, so neither applies to it.
    - A Given written in prose has its check like any other (Examples, Example 38). Whether the Given is right is not yours to judge (Never N6): its check decides that.

  In informed mode the infrastructure has compared each of these already (Dossier guide D32): do not accuse what `auditor.findings` names.

## 13. Argument diagnosis (every receipt of a delivered report)

- **I99 Read the argument diagnosis.** For every delivered report, read the receipt's `named_diagnostics` and each outcome's `named_diagnostics`, `diagnosis_incomplete` and `release` (Dossier guide D33 to D36). A diagnosis is the translator's deterministic record of how the answer argues, cites or calculates, never your verdict, and never a fault you must repeat: the receipt that carries it is right to. Look for three things:
  - **A diagnosis the receipt lacks.** A diagnostic shown at warning or error on a material claim, in an outcome or at the report level, with no INT-ARG-001 whose message starts with its label and code; a finding that carries a label (its `defect`) whose diagnostic the receipt's `named_diagnostics` do not hold; or a diagnostic there at a lower severity than in the outcome, with its cap lifted or its label changed: `finding_missing`. The infrastructure derives the receipt's diagnosis again from what the channel checked (OVS-RCPT-004).
  - **An outcome whose diagnosis or release does not follow from its result.** An outcome whose `named_diagnostics` lack a diagnostic its signed result holds, or show one at a lower severity; whose `release` is `permitted` while a diagnostic at effective warning or error stands, or while its result's `argument_rules` is absent, incomplete or not argdiag-2; or whose `diagnosis_incomplete` is empty while a rule did not finish: `receipt_altered` (OVS-TRN-007).
  - **Records, which are not faults.** ARG-LINK-001 (a link of the writer's own that the proof needs), ARG-LINK-002 (a rule stated from subject knowledge), ARG-FACT-001, ARG-PART-001, EVD-SRC-002, EVD-SRC-004, EVD-LACK-002, ST-STEP-002 and every capped diagnostic are records: they block release at most, and they accuse no one. Never accuse a specialist, a receipt or a translation for carrying one. A diagnostic at warning or error names a defect of the encoding as deterministic code reads it, not proof that the answer is wrong; when you believe a diagnostic misreads the answer, use `other` (When unsure U3): no check decides it.
